The three numbers that decide if you get blocked

A CDN reads your fingerprint before your HTTP request even arrives. A matching User-Agent does nothing if the handshake underneath says "library", not "browser".

JA3MD5 over the TLS ClientHello. Trap: Chrome ≥110 shuffles its extension order every connection, so its JA3 is non-deterministic by design. A fixed Chrome JA3 matches almost nothing.
JA4Same idea, but extensions are sorted before hashing — so it's stable across connections. This is the one to match.
Akamai h2The HTTP/2 fingerprint: SETTINGS, window update, stream priority, pseudo-header order. Catches you even with perfect TLS — most HTTP libraries get it wrong.

What's different here

Pure Go & RustByte-exact browser TLS with no headless browser and no CGo (impersonate-http, impersonate-rs via BoringSSL).
Both sidesThe sword and the shield — emit a fingerprint (impersonate-*) and detect one (ja4-guard).
Measured, not guessedParity tables and datasets come from real handshakes against a live reflector (fp.shshbshsnang.shop), with provenance.
Honest about limitsEvery README says what each tool does and does not do — a careful spoofer still beats the guard, and we say so.
